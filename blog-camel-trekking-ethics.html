Camel Trekking in the Sahara: Is It Ethical?

A fair question, and one worth a direct answer rather than a defensive one.

6 min read · Culture

This is a fair question, and one more travelers are asking directly rather than assuming the answer to. Camel trekking is central to the Sahara tourism experience in Morocco, and like any activity involving working animals, it's worth understanding rather than taking for granted.

What responsible camel care actually looks like

  • Rest days and load limits. Camels used for short tourist treks (45-60 minutes, once or twice a day) are working under far lighter loads than camels historically used for long-distance caravan trade — a well-run operation rotates animals and doesn't run the same camel back-to-back all day.
  • Visible condition. Healthy working camels should look well-fed, without visible sores from saddles or ropes, and should move without obvious signs of distress or lameness.
  • Reasonable rider weight and trek length. Short tourist treks across relatively flat dune terrain are a different physical demand than multi-day desert crossings — this is part of why the standard tourist trek is kept to under an hour each way.

What to ask before you book

A direct question to your operator — how the camels are cared for, whether they get rest days, who owns and manages them — is a reasonable thing to ask, and a fair operator should be able to answer specifically rather than just reassure vaguely. See our guide to booking without getting scammed for more on what a legitimate operator should be able to tell you clearly.

A specific answer beats a general one. "Our camels are well cared for" is a marketing line. "Our camels work half-day shifts and rotate with a second group" is a specific, checkable claim — the difference matters when you're deciding who to trust.

If you'd rather skip it

You don't have to ride a camel to experience the dunes — a 4×4 transfer covers the same ground in about 15-20 minutes, with the same sunset or sunrise arrival at camp. See our full guide to doing the trip without camel riding for exactly how to request it, no questions asked.

Quick answers

A few follow-up questions travelers ask about this.

Are the camels used on tourist treks the same ones used for long-distance work?

Generally no — camels kept for short daily tourist treks are a different working pattern than historic long-distance caravan camels, with shorter routes and lighter, more frequent loads.

Is it better to choose a smaller, local operator for this reason?

Not automatically — size isn't a reliable indicator of animal care either way. Asking direct, specific questions matters more than the size of the operator.

Can I request to walk alongside instead of riding?

Yes, on a private tour this is usually easy to arrange for part or all of the trek — mention it when you book.
