Private vs Shared Desert Tour: Which Is Actually Worth It?

Same route, same dunes, different vehicle. Here's what changes in practice — and when the price difference is worth paying.

5 min read · Comparison

Every Marrakesh–Merzouga route comes in two versions: a shared minibus with other travelers, or a private vehicle just for your group. The destinations are identical — the difference is entirely in how you get there, and that difference is worth understanding before you book, not after.

The case for shared

Shared tours exist because sharing the vehicle genuinely lowers the cost per person — the fuel, driver, and often the accommodation are split across the group. If you're traveling solo or as a couple on a tighter budget, this is usually the more sensible choice, and it comes with a real upside beyond the price: a group of strangers on day one is often a small group of friends by the campfire on night two. Solo travelers in particular tend to enjoy this more than they expect to.

The trade-off is the schedule. Stops are timed for the whole group, so you won't linger an extra twenty minutes at a viewpoint because the light is good, and pickup times are fixed rather than built around your morning.

The case for private

A private vehicle removes the schedule entirely. Want to skip the studio tour in Ouarzazate and spend that time in Aït Ben Haddou instead? Want a 6am start instead of 7:30? Traveling with young kids, older parents, or anyone who needs a different pace? Private solves all of it, because the itinerary answers to your group and no one else's.

It's also the only option if your dates don't line up with a shared departure, or if you'd simply rather not share a long drive with people you've never met.

A middle ground exists. Our shared tours cap groups at a small size rather than filling a full-size coach, and even on a shared vehicle you keep a private tent or room at every stop — you only share the transportation, never your sleeping space.

How to decide

  • Choose shared if: you're solo or a couple, budget matters more than a flexible schedule, and you don't mind (or would enjoy) traveling with others.
  • Choose private if: you're a family or small group already, your dates are fixed, you want control over pacing, or you're combining stops that a group schedule wouldn't accommodate.

Group size changes the math too — for four or more travelers, a private vehicle often costs about the same per person as shared once you split the vehicle cost across the group, which is worth asking about directly when you request a quote.

Shared departures typically run several times a week on the 3-day loop rather than daily, so if your dates are fixed, it's worth confirming availability early rather than assuming a shared spot will be open on short notice.

Quick answers

A few follow-up questions travelers ask about this.

How many people are typically in a shared group?

Usually somewhere between 4 and 12 travelers, kept intentionally smaller than a full-size tour coach.

Can solo travelers request a private car at the shared price?

No — private pricing reflects the full vehicle cost regardless of group size, though the per-person cost drops as more people share it.

Is private always more expensive than shared?

Usually yes for solo travelers and couples, but for groups of four or more it can end up close to the same per-person cost once the vehicle is split.
