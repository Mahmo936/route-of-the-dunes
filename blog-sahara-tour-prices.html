What the extra days actually buy

A 5-day crossing is not a different desert. The higher number comes from two more days of vehicle, driver-guide, hotels and camp nights, plus a slower pace through the kasbah route. If your priority is the dunes themselves, a 3-day loop already reaches Erg Chebbi; if you want the drive spread out with more stopovers, the 5-day version is the one to price.

Compare inclusions, not just the headline. Breakfast and dinner are usually included; lunches, water, local guide fees, quads and tips are usually not. Two identical prices can still cover different things.

How to read a quote before you pay

  • Ask whether the number is per person or per vehicle. Solo travelers and couples pay more per person because the car cannot be split.
  • Ask which camp tier is written in. A standard tent and an ensuite luxury tent can sit in the same camp at very different prices.
  • Ask for inclusions in writing. Water, lunch vouchers, local guide fees and camp transfers should be listed, not assumed.
  • Check the season. Spring and autumn sit at the higher end of each range; summer and winter can run cheaper.

Quick answers

The questions that come up most often when comparing prices.

Are these prices per person or per vehicle?

Per person, based on a small group of 4–6 travelers sharing a private vehicle. Solo travelers and couples typically pay a supplement; larger groups often pay less per person.

Why do published 5-day prices differ between operators?

Group size and inclusions matter more than the route. Published examples are €650 per person for two travellers from one operator, and €580 for two falling to €350 per person for groups of 14–17 from another. Check lunches, entrance fees and camp transfers before comparing.

Is a 5-day tour worth more than a 3-day tour?

A 5-day crossing buys two extra days of vehicle, guide and accommodation, not a different desert. It suits travelers who want a slower pace and more stopovers; a 3-day loop covers the same Erg Chebbi core.

Can I pay for extras by card at the dunes?

No. Roadside stops, camp extras, quad rentals and tips are paid in cash in Moroccan dirhams. ATMs become unreliable after Ouarzazate, so withdraw before leaving Marrakesh or Fes.
