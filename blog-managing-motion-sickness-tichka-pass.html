Motion Sickness on the Tizi n'Tichka Pass: A Practical Guide

The road from Marrakesh to the Sahara crosses the High Atlas via the Tizi n'Tichka pass—a stretch of road defined by thousands of tight curves that demands some practical preparation.

6 min read · Planning

If you are prone to motion sickness, the first day of a desert tour from Marrakesh requires some intentional planning. The N9 highway climbing over the High Atlas mountains via the Tizi n'Tichka pass rises to 2,260 meters, and while the engineering is solid, the route is essentially a continuous sequence of switchbacks for roughly two to three hours.

We drive this route weekly with travelers of all experience levels. Some handle it without a second thought; others turn pale before we even reach Telouet. We prefer to address this directly rather than pretending the drive is a casual Sunday cruise. Here is how to manage the terrain so you arrive in the desert ready to enjoy it rather than recovering in your tent.

Understand the Terrain and the Timing

The most intense part of the drive happens early in the journey. Shortly after leaving Marrakesh, the road begins its sustained ascent into the mountains. You will encounter climbing turns, sudden drop-offs, and frequent passing maneuvers around slow-moving trucks. This winding stretch lasts until you descend slightly toward Ouarzazate.

Because this section occurs during the first half of day one, any remedies—medication, ginger, or wristbands—need to be active before you leave your Marrakesh riad or hotel. Waiting until you feel nauseous on the road is generally too late.

  • First 3 hours out of Marrakesh are the most challenging
  • Road flattens significantly after passing Ouarzazate
  • Return journey from Merzouga follows the same route

Seating Choices and Vehicle Dynamics

In a standard tourist minivan or private 4x4, where you sit matters considerably. The back row of a larger van experiences the most lateral sway and bouncing, which accelerates motion sickness. If possible, secure a seat in the front passenger seat or the middle row.

Keep your eyes on the horizon rather than looking down at a phone, book, or camera. Reading in a moving vehicle on a mountain pass is a fast track to dizziness. Ask your driver to crack a window for fresh airflow, as stagnant cabin heat worsens nausea quickly.

  • Front seat offers the most stable view and least sway
  • Avoid reading, scrolling phones, or reviewing guidebooks
  • Ask the driver for fresh air circulation

Medication and Natural Remedies

Over-the-counter motion sickness medications, such as dimenhydrinate (Dramamine) or meclizine, are effective for many travelers, but they can cause drowsiness. If you take them, keep in mind you might feel sleepy during the afternoon stops at Ait Benhaddou.

If you prefer to avoid pharmaceuticals, ginger chews or peppermint oil can help take the edge off mild queasiness. Eat a light breakfast before departure—avoiding heavy, oily tagines or excessive coffee—so your stomach isn't completely empty, but also not overloaded.

  • Take medication 30 minutes before departure from Marrakesh
  • Opt for a light, non-greasy breakfast
  • Pack ginger tea, mints, or motion sickness bands

Communication with Your Driver

A good driver watches for signs of discomfort, but they cannot read your mind. If you feel your stomach turning, speak up immediately. Our drivers are accustomed to pulling over safely at viewpoints or wide shoulders so passengers can step out, get fresh air, and reset.

There is no shame in asking for a brief pause. Pushing through severe nausea usually ends poorly for everyone in the vehicle. The schedule on day one has enough built-in flexibility for short, unscheduled comfort stops.

  • Tell your driver immediately if you feel dizzy
  • Drivers know safe spots to pull over for fresh air
  • Short breaks prevent full-scale illness

Quick answers

A few follow-up questions travelers ask about this.

Are there restrooms along the Tizi n'Tichka pass?

Yes, there are cafes and viewpoints with facilities near the summit and at major stops like Telouet turnoff, though quality varies.

Will I get sick on the whole 3-day drive?

Usually only the first morning crossing the High Atlas is difficult. Once you pass Ouarzazate heading toward the desert, the roads become much straighter and flatter.

Should I take the bus instead of a tour van?

Public buses take longer and bounce more on mountain passes. Private 4x4s or small tour vans offer a smoother ride and more control over stop frequency.
