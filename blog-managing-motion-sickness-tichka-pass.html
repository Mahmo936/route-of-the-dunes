Motion Sickness on the Tizi n'Tichka Pass: What Actually Helps

Seat choice, timing, remedies, and a few honest words about the drive over the High Atlas.

4 min read · Safety

If you are prone to motion sickness, the first morning of a desert tour from Marrakesh needs some planning. The N9 climbs to 2,260 meters over the High Atlas, and the Tizi n'Tichka pass is essentially a continuous sequence of switchbacks for two to three hours.

We drive this route weekly with travelers of all ages. Some are fine; others turn pale before Telouet. Here is what actually helps, rather than pretending the drive is a casual cruise.

Understand the terrain and the timing

The hardest part comes early. Shortly after leaving Marrakesh, the road starts climbing: tight turns, sudden drop-offs, and frequent passing around slow trucks. The winding section lasts until you begin the descent toward Ouarzazate, after which the road flattens considerably.

Because this happens on the first morning, any remedy — medication, ginger, or wristbands — has to be active before you leave the riad. Waiting until you feel sick on the road is usually too late.

  • The first three hours out of Marrakesh are the most difficult.
  • The road flattens after Ouarzazate.
  • The return journey uses the same pass, so prepare again.

Choose your seat and manage the cabin

In a full tourist van, the back row amplifies every sway and bump. Ask for the front passenger seat or the middle row. Looking forward through the windshield helps your brain reconcile the motion your body feels with what your eyes see, so put away phones, books, and maps until you reach flatter ground.

Ask the driver to crack a window. Stale, hot cabin air makes nausea worse quickly, and a little airflow helps more than most people expect.

  • Front seat or middle row, never the back row of a large van.
  • Eyes on the horizon, not on a screen.
  • Fresh air circulating through the cabin.

Medication and natural remedies

Over-the-counter tablets such as dimenhydrinate (Dramamine) or meclizine work for many travelers, but they can cause drowsiness — worth knowing if you plan to walk around Ait Ben Haddou in the afternoon. Take the dose 30 to 45 minutes before departure so it is active when the climbing starts.

If you prefer to avoid medication, ginger chews, ginger tea, peppermint, and acupressure wristbands help some people take the edge off. Small, regular sips of water are better than large gulps.

  • Take anti-nausea medication 30 to 45 minutes before leaving.
  • Carry ginger chews or hard candy in your daypack.
  • Moroccan pharmacies sell common anti-nausea tablets over the counter.

Eat light before the drive

An empty stomach can make nausea worse, but a heavy, greasy breakfast is equally risky. Plain bread, a banana, or simple porridge works well. Avoid heavy dairy, strong spices, and too much coffee before the pass.

Our drivers stop at roadside cafes along the Tizi n'Tichka for stretch breaks, restrooms, and fresh air. Step outside at every stop, even if you feel fine, and tell the driver immediately if a wave of nausea starts. They are used to pulling over safely.

Vehicle choice: van versus 4x4

Larger 17-seater vans have a higher center of gravity and sway more on tight hairpins. Smaller 4x4s often feel a little more stable, but driving style matters far more than the vehicle. A patient driver who anticipates turns and avoids sudden braking will do more for your stomach than any upgrade. Our van versus 4x4 guide covers the trade-offs in detail.

Talk to your driver

A good driver watches for discomfort, but they cannot read your mind. Speak up at the first sign of dizziness. There are safe shoulders and viewpoints along the pass, and the day-one schedule has enough flexibility for a short unscheduled stop. Pushing through severe nausea rarely ends well for anyone in the vehicle.

Quick answers

A few follow-up questions travelers ask about this.

How long does the winding section of the Tizi n'Tichka pass last?

The most intense climbing and descending lasts roughly two to three hours out of the total drive from Marrakesh toward the desert.

Can I buy motion sickness medication in Marrakesh pharmacies?

Yes. Pharmacies in Marrakesh are well stocked and sell common anti-nausea tablets over the counter without a prescription.

Are 4x4s better than vans for preventing car sickness?

Smaller vehicles often feel more stable, but the driver's pacing matters more. A smooth, calm driver makes a bigger difference than the vehicle type.

Are there restrooms along the Tizi n'Tichka pass?

Yes. Cafes and viewpoints near the summit and at major stops have facilities, though the quality varies.

Will I be sick for the whole three-day drive?

Usually only the first morning crossing the High Atlas is difficult. After Ouarzazate the roads become straighter and flatter.
