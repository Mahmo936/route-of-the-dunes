Camel, Quad or Buggy — and Sunrise or Sunset? How to Choose

Two separate decisions, not one. Here's how each activity and each time of day actually feels, so you can pick on purpose.

6 min read · Activities

Booking your dune crossing means making two independent choices: what you cross the sand on, and when. They don't have to match any particular way — a quad at sunrise is a completely different morning than a camel at sunset, and neither is more "correct" than the other. Here's what actually changes with each.

Camel, quad or buggy

Camel TrekQuad BikeDune Buggy
PaceSlow, guidedSelf-driven, moderateSelf-driven, fast
EffortNone — you just rideActive, easy to learnActive, more physical
Duration45–60 min each way30–45 min session30–45 min session
CostIncluded in every routeFrom €35, add-onFrom €55, add-on
Best forThe classic, quiet experienceTravelers who want to drive themselvesThrill-seekers, small groups

The camel trek is what's included by default, and it's genuinely the right choice for most travelers — it's quiet, it's traditional, and it lets you actually look around instead of concentrating on driving. The quad and buggy are worth adding if you'd rather be active than seated, or if you're combining the dunes with a second night and want a different way to spend part of it (see our note on whether one night is enough).

Not keen on the camel specifically? A 4×4 transfer into camp is always available too — see our guide to skipping the camel ride for how that works and how to request it.

Sunrise or sunset

SunsetSunrise
FeelWarm light, arrival, social evening aheadCool, quiet, camp already asleep around you
TemperatureCooling down from the day's heatColdest part of the day — bring a layer
Wake-upNormal scheduleEarly — typically 45–60 minutes before sunrise
What followsDinner, campfire, musicBreakfast, then departure
PhotographyWarmer tones, more hazeSharper light, fewer footprints in the sand

Sunset is the more common choice, mostly because it lines up naturally with arrival — you drive in, cross the dunes as the light turns gold, and settle into camp for dinner. Sunrise takes more discipline (that early wake-up is real), but it rewards it: the dunes are untouched by the day's foot traffic, the light is sharper, and there's a stillness to camp before everyone else is up.

Staying two nights? This is where it's worth doing both — a sunset crossing on arrival, then a sunrise activity on your free day, so you get the full range rather than picking one.

How to put it together

  • First-timers, one night: camel trek at sunset — the classic combination, no decisions to second-guess.
  • Photographers: sunrise, on either camel or quad — the light matters more than the activity here.
  • Active travelers: quad or buggy at sunset, when temperatures are more comfortable for anything physical.
  • Two nights at the dunes: sunset camel on arrival, then sunrise quad or buggy on your free day.

Whatever you pick, you can choose the activity and the time of day separately when you book — there's no fixed pairing, and you're not locked in until you tell us.
