Sahara Weather by Month (Erg Chebbi)

A compact planning table from our own route notes — with a clear boundary between observed ranges and official statistics.

6 min read · Planning

Spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November) are the most comfortable months: daytime highs in the high 20s to low 30s°C, with nights around the mid-teens. Summer regularly passes 40°C by day but still cools after sunset; winter days sit in the high teens to low 20s°C, and nights can touch freezing. These are practical planning bands from our own route guide, not readings from an official Erg Chebbi weather station.

What to pack by season

  • Spring and autumn: breathable long sleeves for sun protection, a light mid-layer for the camp evening, and closed shoes you do not mind filling with sand.
  • Summer: high-SPF sunscreen, a category 3–4 sunglass, a scarf or buff for wind and sand, and at least 1.5L of water per person on the road.
  • Winter: a real warm layer, a windproof jacket for mornings near 0°C, and socks that can handle both dusty days and cold nights.

The full list is in our free Sahara packing checklist, with an embeddable version and a printable PDF.

Desert nights stay cool year-round. Even in peak summer, temperatures at Erg Chebbi drop after sunset. A light layer is worth packing whatever month you travel.

Quick answers

The questions that come up most often before choosing dates.

What are the best months for the Sahara?

Spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November) are the most comfortable: daytime highs in the high 20s to low 30s°C and nights around the mid-teens. Late March and early October are the sweet spots.

Can I do a Sahara tour in summer?

Yes, with early starts and an air-conditioned vehicle for the driving portions. Daytime highs in the desert stretches regularly pass 40°C, but evenings at the dunes still cool down noticeably.

How cold are Sahara nights in winter?

December and January nights can approach freezing. Camps provide extra blankets and heaters, but a genuine warm layer is worth packing.
