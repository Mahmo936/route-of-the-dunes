How Much Does a Morocco Desert Tour Actually Cost?

From shared budget trips to private luxury camps — here's what actually sets the price tiers apart, not just the numbers.

7 min read · Planning

Desert tour prices in Morocco span a wide range — you'll see everything from €90 to well over €300 per person for what looks, on paper, like the same 3-day trip. The gap isn't random. It comes down to a handful of specific factors: shared or private vehicle, camp tier, and group size. Here's what actually goes into the number.

What actually drives the price up or down

  • Shared vs private vehicle. This is usually the single biggest factor — splitting a vehicle across a group of strangers cuts the per-person cost significantly compared to booking it privately for just your group. See our private vs shared comparison for the fuller trade-off.
  • Camp tier. A shared-bathroom standard tent and a luxury ensuite tent can sit at the same GPS coordinates in the same camp, but the price gap between them is real — private bathroom, hot water on demand, and better bedding all cost the operator more to provide.
  • Group size. On a private tour, four people splitting one vehicle pay less per person than a solo traveler or couple booking the same car.
  • Season. Peak months (spring and autumn) often carry slightly higher rates than the shoulder seasons, simply on demand.
  • Route length. A 5-day crossing naturally costs more than a 3-day loop, mostly from the extra days of vehicle, guide and accommodation rather than anything more exotic.
Watch for what's excluded, not just the headline price. Lunch and drinks are typically not included on any tier — that's normal, not a hidden fee, since it lets you choose where to eat along the route. Ask specifically what's included before comparing two quotes side by side.

Is the luxury tier worth the jump?

It depends what you're optimizing for. If a private bathroom and a proper hot shower after a dusty day matter to you, the luxury tier earns its price. If you're comfortable with a shared bathroom for one or two nights and would rather put the difference toward more travel days elsewhere in Morocco, the standard tier delivers essentially the same experience — same dunes, same camel trek, same sky full of stars.

What's typically included at each tier

Across most operators, the base inclusions look similar regardless of tier: transport, a driver-guide, accommodation at each stop, and the meals listed on the itinerary (usually breakfast and dinner). What changes between tiers is the quality layered on top of that base — a shared minibus versus a private 4×4, a standard tent versus one with its own bathroom, a fixed group schedule versus a pace built around your group specifically. Two quotes with an identical price can still differ meaningfully here, so ask for the inclusions list, not just the headline number.

Costs worth asking about before you book

  • Airport pickup. Some operators include it in the quoted price, others charge separately — worth confirming either way.
  • Optional activities. Quad bikes and dune buggies are almost always priced as add-ons rather than included in the base tour, typically an extra €30–55.
  • Single-traveler supplements. Solo travelers on a private tour usually pay a supplement over the per-person price quoted for a group, since the vehicle cost doesn't split.
  • Tipping. Not mandatory, but customary for drivers and guides — budgeting roughly €5–10 per day per traveler is a reasonable norm on this route.

None of these are hidden fees in the deceptive sense — they're just line items that don't always make it into the first number you see. Asking about them upfront, alongside the checks in our guide to avoiding tour scams, keeps the final bill matching what you expected going in.

Quick answers

A few follow-up questions travelers ask about this.

Are these prices per person or per vehicle?

Per person, based on a small group of 4–6 travelers sharing a private vehicle — solo travelers and couples typically pay a supplement, and larger groups often pay slightly less per person.

Does the price change with the season?

Yes, modestly — spring and autumn (the most popular months) tend to sit at the higher end of each tier's range, while summer and winter can run slightly cheaper.

Is a deposit required to book?

Most operators, including us, ask for a partial deposit to confirm your dates, with the balance due closer to or on arrival — confirm the exact terms before you pay anything.
