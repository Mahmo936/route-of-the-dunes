Desert Camp Food: Menus, Water, & Dietary Options

A practical look at food logistics, dietary adaptations, and water safety in deep-desert Berber camps.

6 min read · Planning

Preparing food deep in the dunes of Erg Chebbi requires careful supply planning. Fresh produce, meats, and dry goods are hauled across the sand tracks from market towns like Rissani and Erfoud. Camp kitchens rely on propane burners, solar power, and ice chests or limited refrigeration. Despite these off-grid conditions, camp cooks consistently serve hot, wholesome Moroccan meals prepared fresh each evening.

Understanding what camp kitchens can and cannot easily adapt helps you plan ahead. Standard meals lean heavily on Moroccan staples, but accommodating vegetarian, vegan, and gluten-free diets is straightforward if coordinated before your tour starts. Here is an honest breakdown of desert camp dining, water policies, and what to pack if you have severe dietary needs.

Standard Desert Camp Menus: Dinner and Breakfast

Dinner at both standard and luxury camps typically begins with hot soup—either traditional Moroccan harira (lentils, chickpeas, tomato) or a light vegetable broth—served with fresh khobz (Moroccan flatbread) and small warm salads like cooked spiced carrots or zaalouk (eggplant dip).

The main course is almost always a slow-cooked tagine. Chicken with preserved lemons and olives, or tender beef or lamb with prunes and almonds, are standard. Vegetarian tagines feature potatoes, carrots, zucchini, and green beans cooked with cumin and turmeric. Dessert consists of fresh seasonal fruit—pomegranates, oranges dusted with cinnamon, or melon—followed by hot mint tea.

  • Dinner: Soup, bread, warm salads, chicken/beef or vegetable tagine, seasonal fruit, mint tea.
  • Breakfast: Khobz flatbread, msemmen (flaky pancakes), eggs (fried or boiled), butter, jam, cheese, olives, coffee, and tea.

Vegetarian, Vegan, and Gluten-Free Options

Vegetarian meals are easy for camp cooks because vegetable tagines, lentil dishes, and fresh salads are natural parts of Moroccan cuisine. If you notify your driver or booking coordinator ahead of time, vegetarian suppers are provided seamlessly without extra cost.

Vegan and gluten-free diets require a bit more attention. Vegans receive vegetable tagines prepared without smen (clarified butter) or animal fats, though breakfast baked goods are largely wheat- and dairy-based. For gluten-free travelers, tagine sauces are naturally thickened by reduced vegetables rather than flour. However, because bread is the primary eating utensil in Morocco, cross-contamination on kitchen prep tables can occur. For more details on managing diet along the drive, read our guide on dietary restrictions in the Sahara.

Bottled Water Policy and Hydration

Camps supply sealed, commercially bottled mineral water for dining and overnight stays. Camp tap water comes from local desert wells or bulk storage tanks; while fine for quick handwashing, it is high in mineral content and should not be drunk or used for brushing teeth.

Your private driver will ensure you have bottled water stocked in the 4x4 for the drive across the Tizi n'Tichka pass and through the gorges. We recommend picking up an extra 5-liter jug or extra 1.5L bottles at a shop in Rissani before entering the dunes if you consume higher volumes of water, especially during warmer months.

Handling Severe Food Allergies and Strict Needs

Camp kitchens operate in confined spaces with small prep crews. If you have severe Celiac disease, air-borne nut allergies, or strict kosher/halal requirements beyond standard practice, you must plan for self-sufficiency. Camp cooks are cautious and clean, but certified allergen-free isolation is not realistic in a desert environment.

We advise travelers with severe allergies or strict medical diets to bring non-perishable backup food from home or high-end supermarkets in Marrakesh. Vacuum-sealed protein packs, gluten-free crackers, energy bars, and personal utensils ensure you stay well-fed even if camp options feel limited.

Quick answers

A few follow-up questions travelers ask about this.

Is bottled water included with dinner and breakfast at Erg Chebbi camps?

Yes, standard and luxury desert camps provide sealed bottled mineral water for dining and sleeping tent nightstands. Drivers also carry bottled water in the transfer vehicle.

Can desert camps accommodate severe Celiac disease safely?

Camp cooks clean surfaces thoroughly and tagine dishes are naturally wheat-free, but because flatbread is central to the kitchen space, zero cross-contamination cannot be guaranteed. Celiac travelers should bring sealed gluten-free bread or snacks.

Is alcohol served with dinner at desert camps?

Most desert camps do not hold liquor licenses and do not sell alcohol on-site. Guests are welcome to bring their own wine or beer, which can be purchased at major supermarkets in Marrakesh, Ouarzazate, or Erfoud before reaching camp.
