Sahara Luggage Storage: What to Bring & Leave Behind

How to manage your baggage between the vehicle, base hotel, and desert camp without dragging your entire wardrobe through the sand.

6 min read · Planning

When you book a transfer tour from Marrakesh or Fes to the Erg Chebbi dunes, space in the transport vehicle is shared and limited. If you are continuing your travels in Morocco after the desert, you likely have rolling luggage or large backpacks that you simply cannot bring into the dunes.

Most travelers make the mistake of either packing too much for the camp or panicking about leaving valuables in base hotels. Here is how we handle luggage logistics on our routes, what stays behind safely, and what actually belongs in your daypack for the desert night.

Where Does Your Main Luggage Go?

On the morning you leave the paved roads for the sand, your large suitcases or main backpacks stay behind. If you are doing a loop tour that returns to your starting city, you can leave bags at your Marrakesh or Fes riad—most hold luggage for free.

If you are doing a one-way transfer (Marrakesh to Fes or vice versa), your main bags travel in the trunk of the vehicle. They are secure during transit, but once you reach the desert transfer point at Merzouga or Hassi Labied, you will transfer to a 4x4 or camels. Large rigid suitcases cannot cross the sand dunes; they stay at our partner base hotel, where rooms are locked and monitored.

  • Riad storage in Marrakesh/Fes is usually free if returning.
  • Hard suitcases cannot be brought onto camel treks or deep desert camps.
  • Base hotels in Merzouga provide secure storage for bags left behind.

Packing Your Desert Daypack

You only need a small backpack or canvas duffel for the night in the dunes. Think of it as an overnight bag rather than a temporary home. You want enough clothing for the evening chill and the next morning, but nothing excessive.

Keep in mind that fine desert sand gets into everything, including zippers. Avoid bringing delicate leather bags if you mind them getting dusty, and keep electronics zipped inside protective pouches.

  • One change of comfortable clothes for the evening.
  • Fleece or warm layer (temperatures drop sharply after sunset).
  • Basic toiletries, toothbrush, and personal medications.

Valuables and Electronics

Never pack passports, cash, expensive cameras, or laptops into the main luggage that gets left at the base hotel or stored in a riad. Keep your critical documents and electronics in your daypack, which stays with you at all times.

Power banks are essential because standard desert camps run on limited solar electricity, and charging ports can be scarce or shared. A headlamp is also far more useful than your phone flashlight when walking around the camp at night.

  • Keep passports and cash on your person or in your daypack.
  • Bring a high-capacity power bank for phones and cameras.
  • A headlamp helps navigate unlit paths around the tents.

The Dust Factor: Protecting Your Gear

Erg Chebbi sand is remarkably fine—almost like flour. It finds its way into camera lenses, zipper teeth, and charging ports if you are not careful.

Ziploc bags or dry sacks are cheap insurance for your phone, camera lenses, and spare batteries. Shake out your daypack before putting it back into your main luggage after the trip.

Quick answers

A few follow-up questions travelers ask about this.

Can I bring a rolling suitcase to the desert camp?

No. Wheeled suitcases cannot be pulled through deep sand. They must be left at the base hotel in Merzouga, and you transfer your essentials into a daypack.

Is it safe to leave bags at the Merzouga base hotel?

Yes. Reputable operators store bags in locked rooms or secure designated areas at the hotel where you meet your camel guide or 4x4 driver.

What should I wear on the camel ride versus what goes in the bag?

Wear long, flexible trousers (jeans can chafe on camels) and sturdy closed-toe shoes. Pack your warm layers and sleepwear in your daypack.
