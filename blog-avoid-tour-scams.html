How to Book a Desert Tour Without Getting Scammed

Most desert tour problems are avoidable with a few checks before you pay anything — here's what to look for.

7 min read · Planning

Most bad desert-tour experiences aren't dramatic scams — they're smaller disappointments that were avoidable with a few checks before booking: a "private" tour that turns out to be shared, a camp tier that doesn't match the photos, or a deposit paid to someone who disappears. Here's what actually protects you.

Before you pay a deposit

  • Get everything in writing. Route, dates, camp tier, what's included, and the total price — a written confirmation, even a simple WhatsApp message, is enough. A verbal-only agreement gives you nothing to point back to.
  • Check for a real online presence. A legitimate operator running this route regularly should have a website, reviews on Google or TripAdvisor, and a consistent business name across platforms — not just a WhatsApp number from a street approach.
  • Ask about the guide's license. Morocco requires licensed guides — a direct question about this, and a direct answer, is one of the clearest signals of legitimacy. See our Morocco safety guide for more on why this specifically matters on desert routes.
  • Be wary of pressure to pay immediately. "This price is only available if you book right now" is a common pressure tactic — a genuine operator can hold a quote for at least a day or two.

Red flags specific to desert tours

A few patterns come up often enough to name directly:

  1. Vague inclusions. "All meals included" without specifying which meals, or "luxury camp" without a photo of the actual tent, are worth pressing on before you pay.
  2. No cancellation terms. A reputable operator should be able to state their cancellation and refund policy clearly, not deflect the question.
  3. Bait-and-switch vehicles. Confirm private vs shared explicitly — this is one of the most common points of disappointment, and it's an easy thing to nail down before booking. See our private vs shared comparison for what each should actually include.
  4. Unrealistically low prices. If a quote sits well below the typical range for the route, either the camp tier, vehicle, or included meals are being cut somewhere — see our cost breakdown for what a realistic price actually looks like.
Street approaches aren't automatically scams — many legitimate local guides do work this way — but they deserve the same checks as any other booking: written confirmation, a real price breakdown, and no pressure to pay in cash on the spot before you've seen anything in writing.

If something already feels off

Trust that instinct. Ask the specific questions above directly, and if the answers are evasive or the pressure to pay increases rather than the clarity, it's reasonable to walk away and book with someone else — there are enough legitimate operators running this route that you don't need to take the risk.

Booking platforms versus booking directly

Both routes can work well. Booking through an established platform (Viator, GetYourGuide, and similar) adds a layer of buyer protection and review verification, often at a slightly higher price. Booking directly with an operator, as with a local guesthouse recommendation or a website like this one, can mean a better price and more direct communication, but shifts more of the verification work onto you — which is exactly why the checks above matter more in that case. Neither path is inherently safer; the checks you run before paying are what actually protects you either way.

What to do if you've already paid and something's wrong

Act promptly rather than waiting to see if it resolves itself. Message the operator directly and specifically about the discrepancy, with dates and details, and keep a written record of the exchange. If you paid by card or through a platform with buyer protection, most allow a dispute or refund claim within a set window after the service date — check the specific terms of however you paid, since this window can be shorter than travelers expect.

Quick answers

A few follow-up questions travelers ask about this.

Should I pay the full amount upfront?

No — a partial deposit to confirm dates, with the balance due closer to arrival or on the day, is the normal structure; be cautious of anyone asking for full payment far in advance.

Is cash or card safer for paying a deposit?

A traceable payment method (bank transfer, card, or a payment platform) leaves a record that cash doesn't — useful if there's ever a dispute.

What if I already paid and the tour doesn't match what was promised?

Document everything (messages, confirmations, photos) and raise it directly with the operator first; if payment was made via card or a platform with buyer protection, that's your next step if the operator doesn't resolve it.
