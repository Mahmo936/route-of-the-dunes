Things to Do in Marrakech Beyond the Desert Tour

A few extra days in Marrakech before or after the dunes? Here's what's genuinely worth doing with them.

7 min read · Destinations

Most travelers on our 3-day loop spend at least a night or two in Marrakesh before or after the desert — and the city holds up well beyond being just a departure point. Here's what's actually worth the time, not just what every list repeats.

Jemaa el-Fnaa and the souks

The main square is genuinely worth doing twice: once in the afternoon to see the layout and shop the surrounding souks while things are calmer, and again after dark when the food stalls, musicians and crowds transform it into something closer to a nightly festival. The souks themselves reward wandering without a fixed destination — leather, lanterns, spices and textiles are spread across a genuine maze of alleys, and getting slightly lost is part of how it works.

Gardens and museums worth the entry fee

  • Majorelle Garden. A compact, cobalt-blue garden with a small Berber museum, popular enough that booking a timed entry in advance is worth it in peak season.
  • Bahia Palace. A 19th-century palace with tiled courtyards and carved cedar ceilings — one of the more architecturally striking interiors in the medina.
  • Secret Garden (Le Jardin Secret). Smaller and quieter than Majorelle, with a good rooftop view over the medina included in the ticket.

Day trips from the city

The Ourika Valley, about an hour into the High Atlas foothills, is the most common half-day escape — waterfalls, Berber villages, and mountain air without committing to a full desert route. This is a separate trip from our Sahara routes, worth knowing if someone mentions "the Atlas day trip" and you want to check whether it's the same thing as our itinerary (it isn't — our routes cross the Atlas on the way to the desert, rather than as a standalone half-day).

Where to base yourself

Two areas cover most travelers' needs. The medina (old city) puts you inside the action — riads with rooftop terraces, walking distance to Jemaa el-Fnaa, but noisier and easier to get turned around in. Gueliz (the newer town) is calmer, easier to navigate, with more modern restaurants and cafés, at the cost of a short taxi ride into the old city for most sights. Neither is a wrong choice; it depends on whether you want to be immersed or have an easier base to return to.

On bargaining in the souks: it's expected, not optional, for most unmarked goods — a starting counter-offer of roughly a third to half the initial asking price is a reasonable opening, though this varies by vendor and item. Staying good-humored about it goes further than being aggressive.

Quick answers

A few follow-up questions travelers ask about this.

How many days in Marrakech is enough before or after the desert tour?

Two to three days covers the highlights comfortably without feeling rushed — see our guide on how many days you need in Morocco for how this fits into a wider trip.

Is it safe to explore Marrakech on foot?

Yes, in the main tourist areas and souks — the same ordinary precautions apply as any busy city. See our full Morocco safety guide for the specifics.

Which neighborhood is best for a first-time visitor?

The medina if you want to be in the middle of everything and don't mind some noise and navigation; Gueliz if you'd rather have a quieter, easier base and don't mind short taxi rides.
