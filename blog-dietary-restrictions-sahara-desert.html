Eating in the Sahara: Dietary Needs and Food Safety

How we handle dietary requirements and stomach safety across remote mountain passes and desert tracks without making a fuss.

6 min read · Planning

Food is a central part of traveling through Morocco, but when you are six hours from the nearest hospital in the middle of the Sahara, logistics matter. Whether you have celiac disease, a severe peanut allergy, or simply want to know how to avoid traveler's diarrhea, preparation happens well before you reach the dunes.

At routeofthedunes.com, we prefer to be upfront about the limitations of remote kitchens. While traditional Moroccan cooking is generally fresh and adaptable, catering to strict medical diets requires clear communication and a bit of flexibility from both the traveler and the local cook.

Vegetarian and Vegan in the Interior

Vegetables in Morocco are seasonal, locally grown, and genuinely flavorful. Meatless tagines and vegetable couscous are standard staples, making vegetarian travel straightforward once you leave the big cities.

Veganism requires a bit more active management. While tagines are naturally plant-based, cooks sometimes use butter or ghee for flavor, and meat broths can be introduced unless explicitly requested otherwise. Always let your operator know in writing before departure.

  • Vegetable tagines are cooked fresh daily in clay pots.
  • Bread accompanies every meal and is generally vegan-friendly.
  • Couscous is traditionally served on Fridays, but available on request.

Celiac and Severe Allergies

Moroccan cuisine relies heavily on wheat bread, semolina couscous, and barley. If you have celiac disease or a wheat allergy, cross-contamination in desert kitchens is a very real risk, as flour is handled constantly in small workspaces.

For nut allergies, sesame and almonds are common in pastries and occasionally in savory dishes. We recommend bringing your own emergency snacks and translation cards for severe allergies, as remote roadside cafes may not fully grasp cross-contact concepts.

  • Gluten-free grains like rice and potatoes are available with advance notice.
  • Always carry your own emergency EpiPen and antihistamines.
  • Roadside stops have limited options for strict allergen separation.

Water and Food Safety on the Road

Stomach bugs on desert tours usually come from unwashed salad greens or tap water rather than properly cooked hot food. We supply bottled water in our vehicles, but you should also carry purification tablets for emergencies.

Street food in roadside mountain villages can be tempting, but stick to piping-hot items like freshly grilled skewers or cooked tagines. Avoid raw salads, peeled fruit you didn't wash yourself, and ice cubes of uncertain origin.

  • Drink bottled or filtered water exclusively throughout the trip.
  • Wash your hands frequently or use alcohol-based sanitizer.
  • Avoid raw vegetables at remote roadside cafes.

What to Pack Just in Case

Even with the best planning, a sensitive stomach can react to changes in diet, oil types, and heat. We advise packing a basic medical kit with rehydration salts, loperamide, and any personal prescription medications.

High-energy snacks like nuts, dried fruit, or protein bars are useful to keep in your day pack for the long stretches between the High Atlas mountain passes and the Erg Chebbi dunes.

  • Pack oral rehydration salts for rapid recovery from dehydration.
  • Bring familiar snacks for long driving days.
  • Keep personal medications accessible in your packing checklist.

Quick answers

A few follow-up questions travelers ask about this.

Can desert camps accommodate gluten-free diets?

Yes, but you must notify us well in advance. While we can provide rice, potatoes, and naturally gluten-free tagines, separate prep spaces are limited in remote desert kitchens.

Is the tap water safe to drink during the tour?

No. Tap water outside major cities is not recommended for travelers. We provide bottled water in our vehicles for all passengers throughout the journey.

What happens if I get sick during the drive to Merzouga?

Our drivers are experienced with route logistics and know the locations of pharmacies and local medical clinics along the highway between Marrakesh, Ouarzazate, and Erfoud.
