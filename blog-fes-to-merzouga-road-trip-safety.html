Driving Fes to Merzouga: Passes, Checkpoints, and Fuel

A practical guide to navigating the N13 route from Fes to the Erg Chebbi dunes safely and efficiently.

6 min read · Planning

Driving from Fes to Merzouga along the N13 national route covers approximately 470 kilometers and takes between 7 and 9 hours depending on stops and road conditions. The journey moves from northern urban sprawl through the cedar forests of Azrou, over the high-elevation Middle Atlas passes, and down through the arid Ziz Valley into the Sahara.

The road is generally well-paved and accessible for standard two-wheel-drive vehicles. However, it is a single-lane highway in each direction carrying heavy commercial freight, long-distance buses, and tourist drivers. Navigating it smoothly requires understanding local police enforcement practices, mountain weather shifts, and sparse fuel stretches.

Col du Zad and Mountain Pass Weather

The highest elevation point on the N13 is the Col du Zad pass, reaching nearly 2,200 meters between Timahdite and Zaida. Between November and March, winter weather can bring heavy fog, ice, and occasional snowfall that temporarily closes the pass.

Road clearing crews operate regularly, but delays occur. If traveling in winter, check local weather updates before leaving Fes. When conditions deteriorate, snow chains may be required, or traffic may be held at police gates until the road is salted.

  • Check morning forecasts in winter; temperatures drops sharply past Azrou.
  • Maintain extra braking distance on shaded mountain bends where black ice forms.

Navigating Police Checkpoints and Speed Limits

Police checkpoints and speed traps are standard across Moroccan highways. Entering towns like Ifrane, Azrou, Midelt, and Errachidia, speed limits drop sharply from 100 km/h to 80, 60, and eventually 40 km/h. Mobile radar teams are frequently stationed at these transition zones.

Checkpoint locations are marked by blue control signs reading 'Halte Gendarmerie' or 'Halte Police'. You must bring your vehicle to a complete stop at the sign and wait for the officer to give a clear hand motion before proceeding slowly.

  • Always stop completely at checkpoint stop signs, not just a rolling stop.
  • Keep your passport, driver's license, vehicle registration, and rental papers accessible.
  • Fines for minor speeding (MAD 150 to 300) are payable on the spot in cash; demand an official receipt.

Fuel Strategy and Service Stations

While gas stations are frequent near major towns, the 140-kilometer stretch between Midelt and Errachidia has limited options. It is best practice to top off your tank in Midelt regardless of your current fuel level.

Major brands like Afriquia, Shell, and Total accepted credit cards in larger hubs, but smaller rural stations often operate strictly on cash. Keep at least 300 MAD in cash allocated specifically for fuel.

Overtaking and Night Driving Hazards

The N13 handles significant truck traffic moving goods between the coast and the south. Slow-moving vehicles on steep mountain inclines create bottlenecks. Avoid aggressive overtaking; wait for official climbing lanes or clear, straight stretches with full visibility.

We strongly advise against driving this route after dark. Unlit agricultural vehicles, pedestrians on road shoulders, stray livestock, and sudden sand drifting near Erfoud drastically increase collision risks at night.

Quick answers

A few follow-up questions travelers ask about this.

Is self-driving from Fes to Merzouga safe for first-time visitors?

Yes, provided you drive during daylight hours, respect posted speed limits, and exercise patience behind slow commercial trucks. Review our self-drive comparison for deeper context.

What happens if I get pulled over for speeding?

The officer will show you the radar read-out. Fines range from 150 to 300 MAD for standard infractions. Pay in cash and ensure you receive a printed paper ticket/receipt.

Do I need a 4x4 vehicle to drive from Fes to Merzouga?

No. The entire main road route (N13) is paved and suitable for a standard compact two-wheel-drive sedan. A 4x4 is only necessary if you plan to drive directly onto off-road desert tracks.
