Is a Sahara Desert Tour Safe for Solo Female Travelers?

The direct answer: yes, and it's one of the more common trip types we run. Here's what to actually check before you book, with anyone.

6 min read · Safety

This question comes up in almost every conversation with a solo female traveler considering the desert, and it deserves a straight answer rather than reassurance for its own sake: yes, a private or small-group desert tour is a well-established, safe way to see the Sahara solo. Morocco's south sees a steady flow of solo women on this exact route year-round. That said, "generally safe" isn't the same as "every operator is equally trustworthy" — so here's what actually matters when you're deciding who to book with.

What to check before you book

  • A licensed guide, not just a driver. Morocco requires tour guides to hold an official license — ask directly whether your guide is licensed, and don't be shy about it. A legitimate operator will answer without hesitation.
  • Clear communication before arrival. A reply that confirms your guide's name, the vehicle, and the pickup time in writing (not just "we'll sort it out") is a good early sign.
  • Private tent or room, even on a shared tour. As a solo traveler, confirm you won't be asked to share a tent or room with a stranger — on any tour worth booking, this is standard, not a special request.
  • Reviews that specifically mention solo women. General praise is nice; a review from another solo female traveler describing her actual experience is worth more.

What the trip actually feels like

In practice, most of each day is spent in the vehicle with your driver-guide and, on a shared tour, a small group of fellow travelers — rarely a situation where you're truly alone with a stranger for long stretches. At each stop (kasbahs, gorges, the desert camp), there are other tourists and staff around. The desert camp itself is typically staffed by several people, not one, and evenings are social by design — dinner and music around a fire, not an isolated setting.

The parts of the trip that can feel more exposed — after dark at camp, for instance — are also the parts where you're least alone, surrounded by camp staff and other guests.

A joining group. On our shared tours specifically, solo travelers are common enough that you're very unlikely to be the only one — many trips run with two or three solo travelers who end up spending the whole route together.

Practical habits worth keeping

  • Share your itinerary and guide's contact details with someone at home before you go.
  • Keep a screenshot of your booking confirmation and driver's name accessible offline.
  • Modest, breathable clothing works well both culturally and practically in rural stops — loose layers cover more ground than a strict packing list could.
  • A small local SIM card or eSIM keeps you reachable through most of the route, though signal does drop in remote stretches — worth telling whoever you're in touch with at home.

What we'd tell a solo traveler directly

If you're weighing whether to go, the honest framing is this: the risks of a desert tour are mostly the ordinary travel risks — an unfamiliar road, a long drive, unpredictable weather — not risks specific to traveling as a woman alone. The operator you choose matters more than the destination does. Ask the direct questions above, expect direct answers, and book with whoever gives them.

It's also worth saving your guide's phone number in a way that works without data, in case signal drops on a remote stretch of the route — a screenshot or a written note in your bag is enough.

Quick answers

A few follow-up questions travelers ask about this.

Should I tell my riad or hotel that I'm traveling solo?

It's not required, but it's a reasonable extra precaution — most accommodations are used to solo travelers and happy to know your general plans.

What if I feel uncomfortable at any point during the trip?

Say so directly to your guide — a legitimate operator will adjust plans or pace without treating the request as unusual.

Are other solo women commonly on this route?

Yes — solo female travelers are a regular part of the mix on both shared and private departures, not an exception.

Have questions before booking solo?

Ask us anything directly — guide credentials, group makeup on your dates, or tent arrangements. We'd rather answer upfront than have you wonder.
