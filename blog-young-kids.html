Sahara Desert Tour With Young Kids: Is It Doable?

Yes — with a few adjustments to the standard itinerary. Here's an honest look at what's genuinely hard with young kids, and what isn't.

6 min read · Families

Families do this trip regularly, including with toddlers — but the standard 3-day itinerary is built for adult pacing, and a few honest adjustments make a real difference with young kids. Here's what actually matters.

The real challenge: driving time, not the desert itself

The dunes, the camp, the camel ride — none of that is hard for kids, and most love it. The genuinely demanding part is the driving: 5–7 hours on mountain roads is a lot to ask of a five-year-old, let alone a toddler. This is the piece worth planning around, not the desert itself.

What we'd change from the standard itinerary

  • Add a day. Stretching the 3-day loop to 4 days breaks up the two longest driving days, which matters far more with kids than with adults.
  • Build in more stops. A private tour lets you stop every hour or so rather than following a fixed schedule — worth requesting explicitly when you book.
  • Plan around nap times, not around us. Tell your guide your child's usual schedule and the day can flex around it, especially on a private tour.
  • Skip the camel ride if needed. Most camps allow young kids to skip the trek and take the 4×4 transfer instead — see our guide to skipping the camel ride for how to request it.

Heat: manageable, with the obvious precautions

Outside of summer (June–August, which we'd avoid with young kids regardless), daytime temperatures are warm rather than extreme, and evenings at the dunes turn genuinely cool. Sun protection, a hat, and enough water are the same precautions you'd take at any warm-climate destination — nothing specific to the desert beyond that.

What kids tend to love most: sandboarding at camp, the camel ride itself (shorter attention spans handle 20–30 minutes better than a full trek), and — almost universally — the campfire and the sheer number of visible stars, often the first time they've seen a truly dark sky.

What to pack, kid-specific

  • A car seat if you have a young toddler — mention this when booking so the right vehicle is arranged
  • Snacks for the car; meal timing on the road can be irregular
  • A light blanket or layer for the cold desert night, even if the day was warm
  • Any regular medication — pharmacies are sparse between towns on this route

Should you go private or shared with kids?

Private is almost always the better call with young children — a fixed group schedule doesn't accommodate a mid-morning meltdown or an unplanned nap stop, and a private vehicle does. See our private vs shared comparison for the fuller breakdown, but for families with kids under six or seven, we'd steer toward private by default.

As a general guide, most families find the trip works best with kids around four or older, mainly because of the driving time rather than anything about the desert itself — younger children can still do it, but expect to build in more stops.

Quick answers

A few follow-up questions travelers ask about this.

Is there a minimum age for the camel trek?

There's no strict cutoff, but very young toddlers usually do better on the 4x4 transfer instead — see our guide on skipping the camel trek for how that works.

Do young children need their own seat in the vehicle?

Yes — mention ages when you book so the right car seat or booster can be arranged in advance.

Should I get separate travel insurance for kids on this trip?

Check your family policy first; most standard travel insurance already covers children on trips like this, but it's worth confirming before you go.
