What a Night at a Desert Camp Is Really Like

Not just the sunset photo — here's what the hours in between actually look like.

6 min read · Experience

Most photos of a Sahara desert camp show two moments — the sunset camel trek in, and the sunrise over the dunes the next morning. The hours in between are what actually fill the night, and they're worth knowing about ahead of time.

Arrival and dinner

You'll typically arrive at camp in the last light of the sunset trek, with just enough time to find your tent before dinner. Meals are usually served communally in a large dining tent — a tagine or similar Moroccan dish, bread, and mint tea are the standard, regardless of camp tier (see our guide to meals on this route if you have dietary needs).

The evening around the fire

After dinner, most camps gather around a fire for music — drums and, depending on the camp, Berber or Gnawa-style rhythms, sometimes with staff singing or playing rather than a formal performance. This is typically the most social part of the evening, and it's common for shared-tour groups to end the night talking well past when the music stops.

Step away from the fire for the sky. Camp lights make it harder to see the stars from right around the fire — a short walk over the nearest dune ridge, once your eyes adjust, shows the sky at its best. See our guide to photographing the stars if you want to do more than just look up.

Sleeping arrangements

Tents are set up in advance, with real beds rather than sleeping bags on the ground — the specifics depend on your camp tier, from a shared-bathroom standard tent to a luxury tent with its own ensuite. Nights get genuinely cold outside of summer, so expect extra blankets provided regardless of tier.

The morning after

Camps typically wake guests before sunrise for those who want it, with breakfast served afterward. It's an early start, but a short one — most travelers are back on the road within an hour or two of sunrise, whichever route they're on.

Quick answers

A few follow-up questions travelers ask about this.

Is it cold at night in the desert camp?

Often yes, even after a hot day — temperatures drop sharply after sunset, and camps provide extra blankets year-round, more so in winter.

Is there electricity or phone signal at camp?

Limited — some camps have generator power in common areas for a few hours in the evening, but phone signal is generally weak to nonexistent at the dunes themselves.

Do I have to join the campfire evening?

No, it's optional, but it's genuinely one of the better parts of the night for most travelers — you're welcome to head to your tent early if you'd rather rest.
