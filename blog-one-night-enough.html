Is One Night in Merzouga Enough?

The honest answer: it depends what you came for. Here's what one night gives you, and what a second night adds.

5 min read · Planning

This is the question we get asked most after "which route should I book" — and it's really the same question in disguise, since it's the main thing separating the 3-day loop from the 5-day crossing. So here's a direct answer, not a sales pitch either way.

What one night actually gives you

A single night at Erg Chebbi is not a rushed experience — it's a complete one. You'll arrive in time for a sunset camel trek, settle into camp, eat dinner under the stars, sit around a campfire with Berber music, sleep in the dunes, and wake for sunrise before heading out. Nothing about that is cut short. For most travelers, especially those combining the desert with a wider Morocco trip, one night delivers everything the photos promise.

What a second night adds

The honest case for staying two nights isn't "more of the same" — it's a different kind of day in between:

  • A full day with no driving. Every other day of a desert tour involves hours in the vehicle. A second night means one day where the only plan is the dunes themselves.
  • Room for an activity. A quad bike or buggy session fits naturally into a free day, rather than being squeezed between arrival and dinner.
  • Two different skies. Weather and moon phase change what the stars look like night to night — a second night is a second, different chance at a clear, dark sky.
  • Less exhaustion. If you're already tired from days of driving, staying put for a day is often the most restful thing you can do on the whole trip.
What you don't get with a second night: a "better" camp, more included meals per night, or a fundamentally different desert experience. The dunes are the dunes on night one and night two alike — the difference is entirely in having an unhurried day between them.

Who one night suits

  • First-time visitors doing a wider Morocco itinerary with limited days
  • Travelers prioritizing Marrakesh, Fes, or the coast over extra desert time
  • Anyone on the 3-day loop who wants the classic highlights without extending their trip

Who a second night suits

  • Photographers who want a second sunset and sunrise to work with
  • Travelers who find long driving days tiring and want a genuine rest day
  • Anyone planning to add a quad bike or buggy session without rushing it
  • Couples or groups treating the desert as the main event of the trip, not a stop along the way

In practice, this is exactly the difference between our two routes: the 3-day loop is built around one night at the dunes, and the 5-day Fes crossing is built around two. Neither is the "upgraded" version of the other — they're suited to different trips.
